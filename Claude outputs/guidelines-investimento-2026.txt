Fund positioning & guidelines (applicable to all tickets)

1. Strategic scope
Core investments should remain linked with the wine value chain (viticulture → winemaking →
production → supply chain → sales/marketing).
Adjacencies are only acceptable if the solution/technology/process is applicable and relevant for
the wine business or there is a credible transfer plan to a wine use case. Adjacencies may represent
up to 30% of the fund’s investments
This will allow broadening “core” beyond production while avoiding generic, non-wine solutions.
2. Wine sector applicability
Consider solutions that have a clear, practical application in the wine industry. Avoid investing in
general-purpose technologies that do not address a specific wine-related need (for example, generic
HR platforms).
However, sector-agnostic solutions, such as those for supply chain, direct-to-consumer (DTC), or
marketing analytics, can be considered if they demonstrate a strong use case or a committed
pipeline/roadmap within the wine sector.
3. Emphasis on Team/ Founders quality
Especially at earlier stages, the most important factor is the founding team’s quality and their ability
to learn and adapt, and the clarity with which they are addressing a real, relevant problem.
A strong, coachable team can drive a project’s success, while a weak or inflexible team is the main
reason to reject an opportunity.
4. Brand investments
Avoid investments in pure-brand-driven startups, as they typically require significant and recurring
capital, which should be a key consideration in the decision‑making process.
Brand-led investments can be considered if backed with an innovative technology and aligned with
strategic goals (e.g., no/low-alcohol with enabling tech).
5. Follow-on guidelines
Keep a follow-on reserve of 25%-30% of the total fund size, to avoid harmful dilution and to back up
bridges and next rounds.
6. Geographies & sourcing:
Prioritize ecosystem presence and proactive scouting across EU wine geographies (France, Italy,
Germany, Spain, UK), expanding beyond inbound applications. Projects based outside EU, may be
considered extraordinarily if there is a very strong direct strategic relevance for Sogrape or
commercial or operational relation with Europe (e.g. team, clients, pilots, or concrete plan for entry
into the EU).

Investment decision outcomes (applicable to all tickets)

Each assessment ends with one of three decisions:

• INVEST: strong strategic fit AND enough evidence (team, traction, round terms, wine use case) to support the proposed ticket. Conditions may still apply (standard due diligence, pilot), but they confirm what the evidence already supports.

• UNCERTAIN: the startup is interesting and has a clear strategic fit, but decisive information is missing to reach a decision, typically financial data (round size, valuation, revenue or funding history with no data available) or wine-specific evidence. An uncertain decision must state exactly what is missing and how to obtain it (e.g., request the deck and round terms, run a pilot). It should not be presented as "Invest, conditional on DD and pilot" when the missing element is the evidence itself.
Example: an adjacency that matches one of Sogrape's 2026 investment/pilot priorities (strong strategic logic), but without enough financial and wine-specific evidence to justify a medium or large ticket, is UNCERTAIN, not INVEST.

• DO NOT INVEST: fails the mandatory criteria for the ticket or lacks strategic fit, even if more data were available.

An uncertain opportunity stays in the pipeline until the missing information is received, and is then re-assessed.

VITICULTURE | Challenge: How might we build early carbon-capture capabilities through low-cost,

VITICULTURE & WINEMAKING | Challenge: How might we map by-products and convert them into

and impact?

frequency, and advocacy?

IC | Discussion Topics and Main Conclusions
Q1. How should we define the split between core investments and diversification
opportunities?
The IC agreed that core investments should remain focused on the wine industry and Sogrape’s value
chain, but the definition of "core" should be broadened to include not just wine production but also
areas like sales, marketing, supply chain, and customer experience. Diversification into adjacent
sectors (e.g., sustainability, circular economy, AI solutions with potential wine applications) can be
considered selectively, but it should not become the main driver of the fund’s strategy. A balanced
split is recommended: maintain a strong emphasis on core wine‑focused investments, allowing only
limited diversification to capture relevant innovation and manage risk.
Q2. How should we define and prioritize our target areas and challenges according to their level
of importance?
Prioritization should be based on strategic alignment with Sogrape’s needs and industry challenges.
The team suggested defining specific challenges (e.g., reducing pesticide use, improving supply
chain efficiency) to guide proactive sourcing. Inbound applications (via website or other channels)
will continue to be reviewed, but with greater selectivity.
Q3. How do we approach projects that are distant from Sogrape´s core business?
The IC agreed to be open-minded but avoid overly generalist solutions (e.g., generic HR tools) unless
there is a clear path to wine industry’s relevance. Early-stage projects without a wine use case should
be evaluated for adaptability and the startup’s willingness to co-develop with Sogrape.
Q4. How should we allocate the funds’ capital?
The fund should aim for a mix of pre-seed, seed and later-stage investments to balance risk and
potential returns. Smaller tickets (e.g., up to 100k) can focus on team and idea quality, while larger
tickets (e.g., above 300k) should require a solid business plan, traction, and clients. The number of
investments should be determined by the size of the fund and the need to reserve capital for followons (suggested 30-40%). Criteria should be clearly documented and differentiated by ticket size.
Q5. How can we increase the number of projects? Should we launch an open call in 2026?
The IC agreed that being more proactive in the ecosystem (attending international events, building
networks, direct outreach) yields higher quality and quantity of deal flow than relying on inbound
applications. Open calls could be considered, but only after building a track record with initial
investments. Defining and publishing specific challenges can also attract targeted startups.
Q6. What type and frequency of reporting and interaction should we have with the IC? Current
reporting and interaction were considered effective, with regular updates and committee meetings.
The group agreed to continue with the current format but to document and share updated investment
criteria and challenge areas for feedback.
